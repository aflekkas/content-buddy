# Short-Form Video Pattern Library

This is the knowledge base Content Buddy draws from when recommending video ideas.
Patterns are organized into four sections: Hook Archetypes, Video Formats, Retention Mechanics, and Niche Playbooks.

---

## Hook Archetypes

### 1. Controversial Take
**Structure:** State a strong, pattern-breaking opinion in the first 1-2 seconds.
**Example:** "I think 90% of startup founders are lying to themselves about product-market fit."
**Why it works:** Triggers comment bait and watch-through from both agreers and dissenters.
**Best for:** Opinion-led creators, niches where debate is common (business, politics, fitness).

### 2. Counterintuitive Claim
**Structure:** Contradict conventional wisdom, promise to prove it.
**Example:** "Waking up at 5am is actually hurting your productivity. Here's why."
**Why it works:** Curiosity gap forces the viewer to stay to resolve the contradiction.
**Best for:** Educational, self-improvement, business creators.

### 3. Before / After Tease
**Structure:** Show the end result first ("I went from 0 to 100k followers in 90 days"), then promise to reveal how.
**Example:** Split screen of abandoned code editor vs. deployed SaaS with MRR counter.
**Why it works:** Viewer needs to stick around to see the mechanism.
**Best for:** Transformation stories, case studies, tutorials.

### 4. Question Loop
**Structure:** Open with a specific question the target viewer is asking themselves, promise the answer.
**Example:** "Why do your LinkedIn posts flop even when they're good? Three reasons."
**Why it works:** Self-identified viewers can't click away; the promise creates an open loop.
**Best for:** Niche educators, B2B, advice creators.

### 5. Pattern Interrupt
**Structure:** An unexpected visual, audio, or verbal element in the first frame that breaks scroll rhythm.
**Example:** Starting mid-action ("I just broke my laptop"), dropping an item on camera, unusual framing.
**Why it works:** The brain's novelty detector fires and pauses the scroll.
**Best for:** Entertainment, vlog-style, high-volume creators.

### 6. Relatable Callout
**Structure:** Name a specific feeling or frustration the target viewer has right now.
**Example:** "If you've ever been ghosted by a prospect after a great call, watch this."
**Why it works:** The viewer feels seen, treats the video as made for them.
**Best for:** Sales, career, dating, parenting, any audience-specific niche.

### 7. Number / List Hook
**Structure:** "3 mistakes that are killing your X" — specific, scannable, promises brevity.
**Example:** "5 ChatGPT prompts that saved me 10 hours this week."
**Why it works:** Low cognitive cost to commit, clear structure sets expectations.
**Best for:** Tips, tutorials, listicles. Works in almost any niche.

### 8. Story Hook
**Structure:** Open with a specific, concrete moment ("Yesterday a client tried to sue me").
**Example:** "Last month I fired my best-performing salesperson. Here's why it was the right call."
**Why it works:** Narrative pulls the viewer in immediately, emotional investment.
**Best for:** Founders, operators, personal brands built on experience.

### 9. Stakes Hook
**Structure:** Frame the information as high-consequence if missed.
**Example:** "If you're running Google Ads and don't know about this update, you're burning money."
**Why it works:** Loss aversion makes viewers stay to protect themselves.
**Best for:** Warnings, platform updates, legal/financial.

### 10. Visual Hook
**Structure:** The first frame is itself striking, no words required.
**Example:** Whiteboard already filled with a diagram, dramatic location, unusual object in hand.
**Why it works:** Grabs attention before the viewer has to process any language.
**Best for:** Creators with strong visual craft, skit/entertainment.

## Video Formats

### A. Talking Head (Single-Take Monologue)
**Structure:** Creator on camera, direct address, 30-90 seconds.
**Strengths:** Fastest to produce, highest authenticity, works for opinion/advice content.
**Weaknesses:** Low visual variety, depends on charisma and script.
**Pairs well with:** Controversial Take, Counterintuitive Claim, Question Loop hooks.

### B. Voiceover + B-Roll
**Structure:** VO narration over visuals (desk work, walking, demos, stock).
**Strengths:** High retention from constant visual change, lets the script carry the message.
**Weaknesses:** Needs b-roll library or shoot day, more editing time.
**Pairs well with:** Story, Before/After, Number/List hooks.

### C. Split Screen Tutorial
**Structure:** Creator on one side, demo/screen on the other.
**Strengths:** Shows and tells simultaneously, great for tech/software/tools content.
**Weaknesses:** Requires screen capture + face cam coordination.
**Pairs well with:** Number/List, Counterintuitive, Stakes hooks.

### D. Reaction / Stitch
**Structure:** React to another creator's take, a news clip, or a piece of content.
**Strengths:** Borrows attention from source, taps into active conversations.
**Weaknesses:** Tied to the original's lifespan, risks being derivative.
**Pairs well with:** Controversial Take, Relatable Callout.

### E. POV Skit
**Structure:** Short acted scene from a specific point of view ("POV: you're in a standup that should've been an email").
**Strengths:** High shareability and savability, humor travels.
**Weaknesses:** Needs writing and performance skill, harder to sell with.
**Pairs well with:** Relatable Callout, Pattern Interrupt.

### F. Day in the Life / Behind the Scenes
**Structure:** Montage of a day, a launch, a shoot, a build.
**Strengths:** Builds parasocial connection, great for founder/creator brand.
**Weaknesses:** Low direct-response, long to shoot.
**Pairs well with:** Story hook, Stakes hook.

### G. Tier List / Ranking
**Structure:** Rank things (tools, people, strategies) S/A/B/C/D.
**Strengths:** Comment bait, bingeable, clear structure.
**Weaknesses:** Takes effort to script, can feel gimmicky if overdone.
**Pairs well with:** Controversial Take, Number/List.

### H. Whiteboard / Diagram Explainer
**Structure:** Creator draws or references a diagram while explaining.
**Strengths:** Feels substantive, high educational credibility, saveable.
**Weaknesses:** Slower pace, needs strong concept mapping.
**Pairs well with:** Counterintuitive Claim, Question Loop.

### I. Screen Recording Demo
**Structure:** Pure screen recording with voiceover, showing software/tools/workflow.
**Strengths:** Minimal production, perfect for tool creators and AI content.
**Weaknesses:** No face-to-audience connection, easy to look bland without pacing.
**Pairs well with:** Number/List, Stakes, Before/After.

### J. Interview / Two-Camera Conversation
**Structure:** Clip from a longer conversation, two angles, captioned.
**Strengths:** Leverages a guest's audience, natural dialogue feels authentic.
**Weaknesses:** Scheduling, editing a long-form source into short clips.
**Pairs well with:** Story, Controversial Take, Relatable Callout.

## Retention Mechanics

### Open Loops
Introduce a question or promise early and only resolve it at the end ("I'll tell you the #1 reason in a second"). The unresolved tension keeps viewers through the middle.

### Pattern Breaks
Change something every 2-4 seconds: camera angle, zoom, caption style, location, prop, gesture. Prevents boredom and resets the attention timer.

### Visual Proof
Overlay data, screenshots, DMs, or artifacts that validate claims. Raises credibility and gives the eye something to scan while the ear listens.

### Callback Structure
Reference the hook at the end ("...and that's why waking up at 5am is hurting you"). Gives a satisfying close and signals watchability to the algorithm.

### List Pacing
For list-based videos, tease the best item last and increase intensity through the list. Viewers stay to see the "big one."

### Caption Density
Tight, large, on-screen captions that match voice rhythm. Short-form is watched on mute by a meaningful chunk of viewers.

## Niche Playbooks

### Founders / SaaS
- Talking Head + Controversial Take about startup advice
- Voiceover + B-Roll for "day of a founder" or launch diaries
- Screen Recording Demo for building in public / product teardowns
- Before/After on revenue, user growth, or case studies

### AI / Tools Creators
- Split Screen Tutorial showing prompt + output
- Screen Recording Demo with voiceover ("5 Claude prompts that...")
- Number/List hook over a tool stack walkthrough
- Whiteboard Explainer for AI concepts (RAG, agents, evals)

### Service / Agency Owners
- Story hook from client work ("a client tried to sue us...")
- Relatable Callout to your ICP ("if you're an agency founder hating discovery calls...")
- Tier list of tools, vendors, frameworks you use
- Interview clips with clients about results

### Personal Brand / Thought Leader
- Talking Head with Controversial or Counterintuitive takes
- Whiteboard Explainer for your frameworks
- Story hook from personal experience
- Stitch/React to industry news with your perspective

### Coaches / Educators
- Question Loop hook targeting the student's pain
- Number/List of tactics in a split-screen or voiceover format
- Before/After from a real student transformation
- POV Skit caricaturing the wrong way vs. the right way

## Recommendation Heuristics

When recommending a video to a creator, ask yourself:
1. **What is their stated goal?** (awareness, leads, authority, sales, audience growth)
2. **What is their niche and audience?** (B2B founder, consumer coach, tech educator, etc.)
3. **What production capacity do they have?** (solo with phone vs. team with editor)
4. **What's already working for them, if anything?**

Then pick: one Hook Archetype + one Video Format + one dominant Retention Mechanic. Explain why those three fit their situation, and write out the beat-by-beat (0-3s hook, 3-10s setup, 10-25s payoff, 25-40s CTA or callback).

Always give a concrete, specific video idea — not a category. "Do a talking head" is useless; "open with 'most AI agencies will be dead in 12 months,' pivot to the 3 survival patterns, close by calling back to the opener" is a video.
